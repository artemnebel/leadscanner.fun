9 Signs a Local Business Desperately Needs a Website

Not every business without a website is a good prospect. Some are barely operating, some are perfectly happy running on word of mouth and have no intention of changing, and some just aren't in a category where a website moves the needle. Pitching all of them wastes time on both ends. Here are the signs worth watching for before you spend a message or a phone call.

1. They have reviews, but no website

A business with 30+ Google reviews and a 4-star average is clearly getting customers already. That means they're operating, they're getting found on Google, and they're just missing the piece that turns that visibility into more calls and bookings. This is close to the strongest single signal there is.

2. Their only online presence is a Facebook page

A business posting regularly on Facebook or Instagram is clearly comfortable being online; they've just never made the jump to an actual site. That's a much easier conversation than convincing someone who's never had any online presence at all that it's worth the investment.

3. They're in a category people search for before buying

Restaurants, contractors, salons, auto shops, and other local services get searched directly before someone calls or shows up. A business in one of these categories without a site is losing customers to competitors who show up in that search with a working website. Categories people rarely search ahead of time (a storage unit, a vending machine route) benefit far less.

4. They've recently expanded or added services

New locations, new service lines, or new equipment are all moments when a business is already thinking about growth, which makes them more receptive to another investment aimed at bringing in more customers. Local news, a new sign, or a "now offering X" post are all clues.

5. Their competitors down the street have a website

If you're scanning a category and most results have a working site except one or two, those outliers are worth prioritizing. They're not just missing a website in the abstract, they're visibly behind specific competitors a customer might be comparing them against right now.

6. They're a newer business (under 2-3 years)

Older, established businesses sometimes lean on decades of local reputation and genuinely don't feel the need. Newer businesses are still actively building that reputation and are usually more open to anything that helps them get found, including a website.

7. Their hours or contact info change often

Frequent updates to a Google Business Profile (hours, phone number, temporary closures) suggest an owner who's actively managing their online presence, not someone who set up a listing years ago and forgot about it. Active management means they're more likely to see a website as a natural next step rather than an alien concept.

8. They rely on delivery or booking apps

A restaurant only reachable through a delivery app, or a service business only bookable through a third-party marketplace, is paying a commission on every order or booking that a $15/month website with a simple contact form or booking widget could reduce. This is an easy, numbers-driven pitch: "how much would you save owning this channel yourself?"

9. They've responded to reviews

An owner who takes the time to reply to Google reviews, especially negative ones, cares about their online reputation. That's exactly the kind of owner who's more likely to see the value in a website that represents their business the way they want it represented, rather than leaving that impression entirely to a bare Maps listing.

Putting it together

None of these signs are dealbreakers on their own, but the more of them a business checks, the better a prospect it is. In practice, the fastest way to apply this filter across dozens of businesses at once is to scan a category and sort by rating and review count first, since that single combination captures signs #1, #2, and #5 in one pass.
