Cold Outreach Scripts That Actually Get Web Design Clients to Reply

Most cold outreach to local businesses fails for one reason: it's obviously copy-pasted. Business owners can tell within a sentence whether you actually looked at their business or just ran a mail merge. The scripts below work because they lean on one specific, verifiable detail — that this business doesn't have a website — and get out of the way fast.

Email: the direct opener

SUBJECT: Quick question about [Business Name]'s website

Hi [Owner Name],

I was looking up [Business Name] on Google and noticed you don't have a website linked to your listing yet. I'm a web designer working with local businesses in [City], and I wanted to reach out before you go with someone else.

A simple site with your hours, services, and a way to contact you can make a real difference in how many people call instead of scrolling past you for a competitor who shows up with a website. I'd be happy to send over a couple of examples and a rough quote, no pressure either way.

Worth a quick chat?

[Your Name]
[Your Phone]

Notice what this does: it states the specific observation (no website), states the specific benefit (fewer lost calls to competitors), and ends with a low-friction ask (a chat, not a contract). That's the whole formula.

Email: the softer, curiosity-led opener

SUBJECT: Noticed something about your Google listing

Hi [Owner Name],

Your business showed up near the top when I searched [Category] in [City] today, which is great, but there's no website linked, so anyone clicking through hits a dead end. I help businesses like yours turn that traffic into calls with a simple one-page site.

Happy to show you what that could look like for [Business Name] specifically. Let me know if you'd like to see a few examples.

[Your Name]

This version works well for businesses that already rank decently on Google Maps; it reframes the missing website as a leak in traffic they're already getting, which tends to land better than "you should have a website" in the abstract.

Phone: the 20-second opener

// PHONE SCRIPT

"Hi, is this [Business Name]? My name is [Your Name], I'm a local web designer. I noticed you don't have a website yet and wanted to reach out before someone else does. I build simple, affordable sites for businesses like yours. Do you have two minutes, or would it be better to follow up by email?"

Always offer the email escape hatch. Some owners genuinely can't talk mid-shift, and giving them a graceful way to say "email me instead" keeps the door open rather than ending in a hang-up.

Handling the two most common replies

// "HOW MUCH DOES THIS COST?"

"It depends on what you need, but a simple site with your hours, services, and contact info usually starts around $[your starting price]. I can put together a specific quote once I know a bit more about what you're looking for. Want me to send over some examples first?"

// "I'M NOT INTERESTED RIGHT NOW"

"No worries at all, thanks for taking the time to respond. If that changes down the line, feel free to reach out. I'll leave my info with you just in case."

Don't argue with a no. A polite exit keeps you top of mind if their situation changes, and word travels fast in small local business circles; being the pushy web designer is a reputation that follows you.
